2 / 9 introduction
The agricultural advisory crisis in India
Why existing systems fail the 146 million farmers who need them most
  • India has 1 agricultural extension worker per 1,000+ farmers — advice is inaccessible at scale (ICAR, 2023)
  • Crop disease is the leading cause of yield loss, yet most farmers diagnose by eye, often too late (FAO, 2022)
  • Soil nutrient deficiencies cost an estimated 20–30% of potential yield annually, yet soil testing adoption remains below 15% (NABARD, 2023)
  • Mandi price information asymmetry causes farmers to sell at 30–40% below optimal price (eNAM report, 2023)
  • Existing AI tools are English-only, text-only, or require smartphones with stable internet — excluding the majority of target users
What AI can offer
  • 24/7 voice-accessible advisory in Hindi and regional languages
  • Instant leaf image disease diagnosis with explainability
  • Soil-sensor-grounded nutrition recommendations
  • Live mandi price integration with sell/hold guidance
  • Crop recommendations tailored to local soil and climate
  • Multimodal input — text, voice, image, sensor data
Why now?
  • LLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini) now strong enough for domain advisory
  • VLMs (ViT, LLaVA) achieve >90% accuracy on PlantVillage
  • Bhashini API enables 10+ Indic language ASR and TTS
  • RAG removes hallucination barriers for knowledge grounding

4 / 9 research gap
What no existing system has built
Five specific gaps identified across the literature survey
G1
No system unifies disease diagnosis (image), nutrition advisory (sensor), crop recommendation, weather, and live market data in a single conversational session Farmer.Chat handles text + KB only · LLMI-CDP handles image only · AgroLLM handles text RAG only
G2
Cross-modal fusion of leaf image and soil sensor data for nutrition deficiency has not been deployed in a conversational advisory context Sensor fusion papers (PMC 2025) work offline — no dialogue interface wraps them
G3
Explainability (Grad-CAM heatmaps, SHAP bars) is generated internally in research papers but never rendered to the farmer in a UI XAI-CROP produces SHAP values as research output — not as a farmer-facing advisory response
G4
No end-to-end voice-to-advisory pipeline exists for Hindi and Indic languages: ASR → multilingual NLP → agronomic RAG → TTS response Indic ASR benchmark (2025) reports WER as low as 16% but no system connects it to an advisory backend
G5
Live mandi price data has never been integrated into the same conversational turn as a crop disease or agronomic advisory response Market agents and agronomic agents exist in separate silos across all surveyed literature
5 / 9 problem statement
Formal problem statement
Indian smallholder farmers face simultaneous, interdependent challenges — crop disease, soil nutrient deficiency, uncertain crop selection, irrigation scheduling, and price realisation — that cannot be addressed by any single AI model or modality. Existing expert systems are text-only, single-agent, single-language, and non-explainable, making them inaccessible and untrustworthy for the target population. No current system accepts voice queries in Hindi, diagnoses disease from leaf images, integrates real-time soil sensor data, and advises on live mandi prices within a single conversational turn, with explanations grounded in verified knowledge sources.
Challenges to overcome
  • Modality heterogeneity — voice, image, text, sensor, price are structurally different inputs
  • Domain knowledge depth — agriculture requires expert-level, crop-specific, region-specific knowledge
  • Hallucination risk — LLMs fabricate crop-care advice with dangerous confidence
  • Language barrier — Hindi and Indic ASR accuracy still sub-optimal for agricultural vocabulary
  • Trust — farmers reject advice they cannot understand or verify
Constraints of the solution
  • Must work on commodity Android device + 3G connection
  • Response time < 4 seconds for cloud, < 8s for offline-cached PWA
  • Knowledge grounded exclusively in ICAR, FAO, CGIAR verified sources
  • Explainability must be farmer-readable — visual heatmaps, not numerical scores
  • Multi-crop: must cover wheat, rice, maize, cotton, tomato, potato and more
6 / 9 objectives
Research objectives
Eight measurable objectives that together close the five identified research gaps
O1 · architecture
Design a multi-agent orchestration system
Build a LangGraph-based orchestrator that classifies intent and routes queries to the correct specialist agent(s) in parallel, with multi-turn memory.
O2 · disease
Train and deploy a disease diagnostic agent
Fine-tune ViT-L/16 with LoRA on PlantVillage + PlantDoc + IP102, integrate AgriChain CoT, achieve >90% top-1 accuracy across 50+ crops, 200+ diseases.
O3 · nutrition
Develop cross-modal nutrition deficiency agent
Fuse leaf image features (ResNet-50) with soil sensor readings (NPK, pH, EC) via a cross-modal MLP to generate fertiliser dose recommendations.
O4 · RAG
Build a hallucination-resistant RAG knowledge layer
Ingest 250k+ ICAR/FAO/CGIAR document chunks into Qdrant with hybrid BM25+dense retrieval, cross-encoder reranking, and LLM-as-judge confidence gating.
O5 · XAI
Surface explainability to the farmer UI
Render Grad-CAM heatmaps on disease images and SHAP bar charts for crop recommendations directly in the React PWA — not as research output but as user-facing UI.
O6 · voice
Implement end-to-end Indic voice pipeline
Integrate Whisper large-v3 (fine-tuned Indic) for ASR and Bhashini TTS for response delivery, targeting WER <20% for Hindi and <30% for 5 regional languages.
O7 · market
Integrate live mandi price advisory
Pull Agmarknet + eNAM price data at 15-min intervals, run Prophet 7-day forecast, and deliver sell/hold/transport advisory in the same session as agronomic advice.
O8 · evaluation
Evaluate on MIRAGE, AgMMU, and expert panel
Benchmark the system on established agricultural AI benchmarks and validate advisory quality with a panel of ICAR-certified agronomists across 5 crop categories.

8 / 9 evaluation
Evaluation framework
How each objective will be quantitatively measured before claiming contribution
Disease diagnostic (O2)
Dataset: PlantVillage test split + PlantDoc
Metric: Top-1 acc · F1 macro · AUC-ROC
Target: >90% top-1 across 200+ diseases
XAI: Grad-CAM IoU vs expert annotation
Nutrition agent (O3)
Dataset: ICAR soil health card DB + synthetic sensor
Metric: RMSE on fertiliser dose · F1 deficiency class
Target: RMSE <5 kg/ha · F1 >0.85
XAI: SHAP stability score across runs
Crop recommendation (O4)
Dataset: ICRISAT district yield + IMD climate
Metric: Top-3 acc · agronomist agreement %
Target: Top-3 acc >85% · agreement >80%
XAI: SHAP feature rank correlation
RAG quality (O4)
Framework: RAGAS evaluation suite
Metric: Faithfulness · context relevance · answer rel.
Target: Faithfulness >0.88 · hallucination rate <5%
Benchmark: MIRAGE · AgMMU eval sets
ASR / voice pipeline (O6)
Dataset: Indic ASR benchmark (10,934 recordings)
Metric: WER per language
Target: WER <20% Hindi · <30% Indic
End-to-end: voice → response latency <4s
Expert agronomist panel (O8)
Panel: 10 ICAR-certified agronomists
Metric: 5-point Likert correctness + clarity
Target: Mean correctness >4.0 / 5.0
Blind evaluation: 200 real farmer queries
Ablation studies: system with all 5 agents vs. single-agent baseline · RAG vs. no-RAG · XAI rendered vs. not · voice vs. text-only
